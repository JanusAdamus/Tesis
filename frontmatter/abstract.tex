This work compares probabilistic models for symbolic melodies represented as pitch-class sequences. Finite Markov chains provide the starting point. Hidden Markov models (HMMs) separate the observed notes from latent contexts, and the hierarchical Dirichlet process HMM (HDP-HMM) expands the potential state inventory. Computational experiments test these formulations and record the conditions needed to reproduce them.

The final experiment evaluates a finite HMM, a truncated HDP-HMM, a variable-order Markov model, and a causal transformer on the same task of predicting the next event. The data are split by canonical work, keeping arrangements of the same piece together. Of the 3,000 sampled PDMX files, 2,933 pieces remained after preprocessing.

At the full training fraction, the transformer obtained a test perplexity of 5.823. The primary-run values were 7.356 for the finite HMM, 7.968 for the HDP-HMM, and 8.022 for the variable-order model. Four audited sensitivity runs changed window overlap, finite-HMM capacity, and the data split one factor at a time. The transformer ranked first in every run. Removing window overlap reduced its margin, whereas the expanded grid improved the finite HMM to 6.984 without locating its optimum.

The transformer therefore had a predictive advantage at the corpus scale studied. A subsequent fixed-configuration benchmark with three repetitions showed that its fit was faster than either HMM and slower than the VOMM. Under the documented pricing scenario, the GPU rate made its estimated cost higher than that of the CPU alternatives. The complete execution established the technological viability of the research prototype on the available equipment. Social viability was not tested with users. No reference annotations were available for phrases, sections, or harmonic functions; structural quality was not compared.
